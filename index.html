Fostering Innovation in Homeland Defense

The Homeland Defense Institute (HDI) convenes military leaders, academic scholars, and commercial operators to analyze contested logistics, critical infrastructure protection, and societal continuity across North America and the High North.

Who We Are

Connecting academia, defense practitioners, and industry to address strategic challenges.

The Homeland Defense Institute (HDI) serves as a critical academic and research bridge, connecting defense professionals, academics, and industry experts to address complex national security and homeland defense challenges. As an entity aligned with the U.S. Air Force Academy (USAFA) and the NORAD/USNORTHCOM J7, we foster the intellectual capital required to defend the homeland in an increasingly contested strategic environment.

Lines of Effort & Research Portfolios

Core focus areas supporting NORAD and USNORTHCOM mission assurance requirements.

Arctic & High North

Examining extreme cold-weather operations, dual-theater contested logistics, deep-water port availability, and rapid multi-modal supply degradation across the high latitudes.

Critical Lifeline Infrastructure

Analyzing cross-sector failure cascades across commercial maritime shipping, electrical power, refined fuels, communications, and acute supply chains sustaining North American defense.

Counter-sUAS & Gray Zone Wargaming

Stress-testing interagency decision frameworks, subsea infrastructure vulnerabilities, multi-domain autonomous probes, and ambiguous attribution dynamics in multinational coalitions.

Lifeline Infrastructure May 2026
Private-Sector Lifelines in the Arctic: Predicting and Assessing Risk to Critical Infrastructure
Julie Kay Roberts, CEM (PSEMA / HDI Project 1)
Analyzes privately operated physical lifeline networks (commercial fuels, energy, food distribution, communications, and multi-modal transport) supporting Arctic homeland defense under contested conditions.

Human Systems May 2026
Continuity of Society in the North: Multi-Modal Resiliency in Arctic Defense
Julie Kay Roberts, CEM (PSEMA / HDI Project 2)
Examines human systems as operational variables in mission assurance, including military families, civilian/contractor workforces, and tribal/Alaska Native community resilience.

Wargaming & Resilience May 2026
Resilience in the Arctic: Interagency Wargaming to Test Mission Continuity and Domain Awareness
Meghan Iverson (HDI Nonresident Fellow / Univ of Chicago)
Evaluates an interagency stress-test wargame simulating compound environmental and contested operational conditions in Alaska, uncovering critical maritime and power chokepoints.

C-sUAS & Gray Zone February 2026
Polar Frontlines: Full Report and Cadet Handbook
Homeland Defense Institute Research Consortium (Cornell)
Investigates full-spectrum drone threats and gray-zone infrastructure probes against Arctic critical facilities, providing a 72-hour operational simulation package for PME and cadet training.

Arctic Domain May 2026
Bridging Arctic Gaps Final Report
DMATS Task Deliverable (DEL 34 TDL 3-071)
Assesses cross-border coordination architecture gaps, communications fragility above 70 degrees North, and logistics constraints affecting bi-national Arctic mission assurance.
